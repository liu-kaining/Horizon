## [New LLM Architectures Innovate with KV Sharing, mHC, and Compressed Attention](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/recent-developments-in-llm-architectures) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A recent analysis details three key innovations in open-weight LLM architectures: cross-layer KV (Key-Value) sharing, mixture-of-heads compression (mHC), and specialized compressed attention mechanisms, all aimed at significantly reducing the computational and memory costs of processing long contexts. These innovations directly address the critical efficiency bottleneck of long-context processing in LLMs, enabling more scalable and cost-effective deployment for applications requiring extensive context windows, which is a major trend in advancing model capabilities. KV sharing, or cross-layer attention, involves reusing the same K and V projections across multiple transformer layers to reduce redundant computations and memory. Mixture-of-heads compression (mHC) dynamically selects only the most relevant attention heads for each input, similar to a mixture-of-experts approach for heads.

rss · Sebastian Raschka · May 16, 11:33

**Background**: Transformers are the dominant architecture for Large Language Models (LLMs), and their self-attention mechanism, while powerful, becomes computationally expensive and memory-intensive for long sequences due to the quadratic relationship with sequence length. The Key-Value (KV) cache is a critical data structure stored during inference that can consume over 80% of memory. Open-weight models are those whose trained parameters are publicly released, allowing the community to study and build upon their architectures.

## [VLDB Paper Proposes SSD Write Optimization Techniques](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09927) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new research paper published in the VLDB 2025 proceedings argues that adopting out-of-place writes is essential for database systems to fully leverage SSD performance and extend their lifespan. This work provides a set of optimizations that collectively reduce write amplification across both the database management system and SSD layers, addressing a key performance and endurance bottleneck for modern storage systems. The paper proposes out-of-place optimizations targeting write amplification, a critical issue where the physical data written to an SSD is a multiple of the logical amount intended, which impacts both performance and the device's lifespan.

rss · Lobsters · May 16, 08:28

**Background**: Write amplification is a phenomenon in SSDs where the actual amount of data physically written to the flash memory is greater than the logical amount intended by the host system. This is often caused by internal processes like garbage collection and the need to erase entire blocks before rewriting, which can degrade performance and reduce the drive's endurance. Database systems use storage engines like LSM-trees that interact with SSDs, and their write patterns can significantly influence this amplification factor.
