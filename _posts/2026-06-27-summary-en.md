## [China completes world's largest superconducting magnet for fusion reactor](https://www.ithome.com/0/969/318.htm) ⭐️ 9.0/10

China has successfully completed and verified the world's largest superconducting toroidal field magnet for a fusion reactor, which measures 21 meters long, 12 meters wide, and 3.3 meters high, weighing 582 tons. Additionally, a high-temperature superconducting central solenoid magnet for a compact fusion device has passed full-parameter performance tests, with its core performance reaching internationally leading levels. This breakthrough establishes a solid engineering foundation for China's fusion reactor construction, significantly enhancing its domestic independent R&D and engineering capabilities in a critical area of future energy technology. It also marks a leadership position in superconducting component development for fusion, with key performance metrics surpassing international counterparts. The toroidal field magnet is the largest superconducting component for the 'Comprehensive Research Facility for Fusion Technology' major national science infrastructure, with its entire production chain achieving full domestic self-sufficiency and control. The central solenoid magnet induces and drives the plasma current, which is crucial for plasma startup and maintaining its confinement shape.

After 80 years, mathematicians have upgraded Paul Erdős's foundational probabilistic method, which uses randomness to analyze and prove properties about networks and graphs. This upgrade to a foundational technique could significantly advance the fields of graph theory and combinatorics, potentially enabling the solution of previously intractable problems and revolutionizing the study of complex networks. The method, known as the probabilistic method, is a nonconstructive approach pioneered by Erdős that proves the existence of mathematical objects by showing a random selection from a class has a positive probability of satisfying the desired property.

rss · Quanta Magazine · Jun 26, 15:26

**Background**: Paul Erdős was a prolific 20th-century mathematician who pioneered the use of probabilistic methods in combinatorics. The probabilistic method is a powerful nonconstructive technique where one proves that a structure with certain properties exists by showing that a randomly chosen object from a relevant class has a positive probability of having those properties. This approach has been fundamental in graph theory, particularly in studying random graphs like the G(n,p) model, which generates a network with a fixed number of vertices.
